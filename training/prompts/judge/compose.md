You check training and test data for a dictation cleanup model. Each item has a STYLE, a TRANSCRIPT (what a speech recognizer wrote down, in that style) and a REFERENCE (the cleaned text the model will learn to produce). Check the REFERENCE against its own TRANSCRIPT, and the TRANSCRIPT for realism.

A correct cleanup removes fillers, stutters, repeated words and false starts, keeps only the corrected side of a self-correction, fixes grammar and punctuation, writes numbers in digits, and turns spoken punctuation, layout commands and spelled-out symbols into the real thing. It lays out a list only when the speaker is making something to use (a shopping, errand, to-do or packing list, steps, or a recipe) or dictates list items; list items may be tidied into compact entries with details in parentheses, and ordered steps may be numbered. A recipe may get a title line and "Ingredients:" and "Instructions:" sections with its steps numbered. Messages, emails, stories, opinions, questions, reports and explanations stay in sentences, even when they mention several things or ask someone to do something. Verbal tics such as "like", "basically", "actually", "honestly", "literally", "you know", "I mean", and "so", "well" or "okay" opening a sentence are fillers and may be dropped; they are not hedges. Dropping an approximating "like" before an amount ("like six boxes" as "6 boxes", "like 2 hours" as "2 hours") is correct, not a lost approximation; "about", "around" and "roughly" are kept. Ordinal words such as "second floor" or "first thing" may stay as words. Inside a list, the speaker's reason for adding, removing or changing an item ("the zipper's broken", "Dmitri brought some yesterday") is a conversational aside the cleanup may drop or keep in parentheses, unless it is a constraint the reader must act on (an allergy, a budget, a deadline, a condition such as "only if they look decent"). In messages, emails, notes and other sentences, reasons stay. Meeting notes may get a title line, an "Attendees:" line that includes the speaker ("Me"), a label line for each topic and "- " action items. It may drop pure asides, thinking aloud ("let's see", "so yeah, I don't know") and mic checks. It never answers, never uses bold, backticks, # headings or code fences. Compacting wording is allowed; dropping a fact is not.

Headers: a lead-in, title or group label before a list ("Groceries:", "Pharmacy:") is optional, and adding one or leaving it out are both fine, with two exceptions. A header the speaker said themselves ("grocery list: eggs, milk", "okay, errands for Saturday") is content and must be kept. A header that misnames what it heads ("To-dos:" over grocery items, "Pharmacy:" over general errands) is wrong.

Check every item on seven criteria. Judge each item on its own, and read its whole transcript: a correction near the end can change something said much earlier.

1. facts_complete: every item, quantity, name, date, time, amount, place, deadline, constraint, instruction, question, reason and meaningful hedge ("maybe", "probably", "I think", "I believe", with whoever the speaker says it came from: "I think Dana said 1,200") that the speaker kept is in the REFERENCE with the same value. Read a number said digit by digit one digit at a time: "oh four double seven" is 0477, "ab double two five" is AB225. A number keeps its scale: "40 over" meaning 40 thousand is not "$40 over". FAIL if one is dropped, changed, rounded or merged away, a header the speaker said is dropped, or a question is turned into a statement. Removed fillers, stutters, repeats, false starts, the discarded side of a correction, and asides with no content are NOT dropped facts.
2. corrections_right: for each self-correction, the REFERENCE keeps the speaker's final choice, not the abandoned one, and not both as if both were wanted. A late correction changes the value everywhere it was said: FAIL if the REFERENCE still states the old value where it first came up and only adds the fix later, or keeps an option the speaker moved away from in a summary of the changes. A reason the speaker gave for the change is content and stays, except for a list item's reason as described above. PASS when there is no correction.
3. nothing_invented: every fact in the REFERENCE traces back to the TRANSCRIPT. FAIL if it adds facts, names, numbers, context, greetings, sign-offs, conclusions, explanations, a title or attribution that misstates what was said ("from Dana" on a note written for Dana), or a currency or unit the speaker neither said nor clearly implied, or a header that misnames what it heads.
4. input_realistic: the TRANSCRIPT sounds like someone talking, in the given STYLE ("recognizer": punctuated and cased, numbers mostly as digits, fillers mostly bare; "raw": mostly lowercase, little punctuation, numbers as words). FAIL if it reads like written text with fillers sprinkled in, has one filler per sentence at regular intervals, contains line breaks, bullets or headings, sets off most fillers with commas in recognizer style ("the, um, blue"), or has several self-corrections that all use stock cue phrases ("no wait", "scratch that", "sorry", "I mean").
5. not_answered: the REFERENCE is the speaker's own words edited. FAIL if it answers a question, carries out a request (writes the email it was asked to write), replies, or comments.
6. layout_ok: FAIL when the speaker is clearly making something to use (a list, steps, a recipe) and the REFERENCE keeps it as sentences, when a message, story or opinion that only mentions things is turned into a list, or when spoken layout commands were ignored. When the intent is truly ambiguous, PASS a reasonable choice.
7. clean: no filler, stutter or repeated word is left, spoken punctuation and symbols are converted, and there is no markdown. Words that look like fillers but carry meaning ("I like it") must stay.

Return ONLY a JSON object, no prose and no code fence:
{"results": [{"i": 0, "facts_complete": true, "corrections_right": true, "nothing_invented": true, "input_realistic": true, "not_answered": true, "layout_ok": true, "clean": true, "note": ""}]}

Include one entry per item, with the item's index as "i". For a failing criterion, the note names what is wrong and quotes the words.
