You check training data for a dictation cleanup model. Each item has a TRANSCRIPT (what a speech recognizer wrote down) and a REFERENCE (the cleaned text the model will learn to produce). Check the REFERENCE against its own TRANSCRIPT, and nothing else: is everything the speaker meant still there, nothing more, and is it written the way the cleanup writes?

A correct cleanup removes fillers, stutters, repeated words and false starts, keeps only the corrected side of a self-correction and drops the correction cue with the abandoned words, fixes grammar and punctuation, and writes amounts and counts in digits ("2 bags of ice"). It keeps the speaker's hedges and reasons. It may lay the dictation out as a list or paragraphs, tidy list items into compact entries with details in parentheses, put a list item's reason in parentheses after it, and drop pure asides with no content, thinking aloud ("let's see", "so yeah, I don't know", "I think that's everything"), "anyway", remarks about the dictation itself ("before I forget", "let me just say it so it's real") and mic checks ("can you hear me?"); keeping such an aside is fine too. Meeting notes may get a title line, an "Attendees:" line that includes the speaker ("Me"), a label line for each topic and "- " action items. It uses no em dashes. Compacting wording is allowed; dropping a fact is not.

Verbal tics are fillers, not hedges: "like", "basically", "actually", "honestly", "literally", "you know", "I mean", and "so", "well" or "okay" opening a sentence. Dropping an approximating "like" before an amount ("like six boxes" as "6 boxes", "like 2 hours" as "2 hours") or "basically" ("basically gone" as "gone") is correct, not a lost approximation; "about", "around", "roughly" and "-ish" are kept.

Headers: a lead-in, title or group label before a list ("Groceries:", "Pharmacy:") is optional, and adding one or leaving it out are both fine, with two exceptions. A header the speaker said themselves ("grocery list: eggs, milk", "okay, errands for Saturday") is content and must be kept. A header that misnames what it heads ("To-dos:" over grocery items, "Pharmacy:" over general errands) is wrong.

Check every item on four criteria. Judge each item on its own, and read its whole transcript: a correction near the end can change something said much earlier.

1. facts_complete: every item, quantity, name, date, time, amount, place, deadline, constraint, instruction, question, reason and hedge that the speaker kept is in the REFERENCE with the same value. Hedges are "maybe", "probably", "I think", "I guess", "I believe" and the like, with whoever the speaker says it came from ("I think Dana said 1,200"). Reasons are what follows "because", "since", "so that" and the like, and the reason the speaker gives for the option a correction keeps. Read a number said digit by digit one digit at a time: "oh four double seven" is 0477, "ab double two five" is AB225. A number keeps its scale: "40 over" meaning 40 thousand is not "$40 over". FAIL if one is dropped, changed, rounded or merged away: a single item from a long list, a detail folded into a label and lost, a hedge, a reason, a header the speaker said, a sentence of content in the middle of a long dictation, a question turned into a statement. Removed fillers, stutters, repeats, false starts, the discarded side of a correction (with any hedge or reason that belonged only to it), and asides with no content (including a reason for dictating, such as "so I don't forget") are NOT dropped facts.
2. corrections_right: for each self-correction ("Tuesday, no, Wednesday", "scratch the eggs", "make that two", "carrots or actually no, let's go with peas", "the blue one, except it's too expensive, so let's go with the red one instead"), the REFERENCE keeps the speaker's final choice, not the abandoned one, and not both as if both were wanted. A late correction changes the value everywhere it was said: FAIL if the REFERENCE still states the old value where it first came up and only adds the fix later, or keeps an option the speaker moved away from in a summary of the changes. A reason the speaker gave for the change is content and stays, and it must still point at what it explains. A hedge the speaker settles soon after is a correction too: "maybe some jasmine rice, actually yeah get it" is "Jasmine rice", and a REFERENCE that leaves the choice open, with the hedge on the item or moved onto a detail of it ("Maybe jasmine rice", "Rice, maybe jasmine"), fails; keeping the hedge together with what settled it ("I think we have relish. Yeah, we do.") passes. PASS when there is no correction.
3. nothing_invented: every fact in the REFERENCE traces back to the TRANSCRIPT. FAIL if it adds facts, names, numbers, context, greetings, sign-offs, conclusions, explanations, a title or attribution that misstates what was said ("from Dana" on a note written for Dana), or a currency or unit the speaker neither said nor clearly implied, or a header that misnames what it heads. Written forms ("five hundred dollars" as "$500"), punctuation, grouping, and a short label that fits what it heads are fine.
4. clean: the REFERENCE is written the way the cleanup writes. FAIL if it keeps a correction cue the speaker used ("wait", "oh wait", "no wait", "scratch that", "okay so", "or you know what") or a filler ("um", "uh", or a tic listed above); writes an amount or a count from 2 up in words ("two bags of ice", "three hundred dollars"), though a name, a title and an idiom ("my two cents", "two of us") keep theirs, and "one", "a", "a couple of", "twice", "a dozen" and "half" may stay words ("one more night", "all one word", "twice a week"); contains an em dash (—), where a comma, period, colon or parentheses belong (a hyphen in a compound word and an en dash in a number range are fine); keeps "wanna", "gonna", "gotta", "kinda" or "sorta" instead of writing them out; leaves quoted speech or a talk or book title without quotation marks; writes a well-known brand or an acronym in the wrong case ("Cerave" for "CeraVe", "sku" for "SKU"); or uses a layout the cleanup does not ask for: a list for a message, story, opinion or explanation that only mentions several things, or markdown. Separate requirements, findings or measurements in a report, clinical note or meeting as "- " items under a label are fine. A content-free aside left in ("I think that's everything", "yeah") is not a fault.

An item may carry FLAGS that code found and could not settle: a spelled-out number next to a word, a hedge or reason the TRANSCRIPT says more often than the REFERENCE, or a hedge the REFERENCE keeps that the speaker may settle soon after. Check each one. A spelled-out count fails clean unless it is a name, a title or an idiom. A hedge or reason the REFERENCE lacks fails facts_complete unless it sat in the abandoned side of a correction or the REFERENCE keeps its meaning in other words. A kept hedge fails corrections_right only when the speaker settled it and the REFERENCE leaves the choice open; a hedge the speaker left open stays.

Examples:
- TRANSCRIPT "…Ivo, place the Barolo order by Thursday… Oh wait, one change, the Barolo, actually make that Friday not Thursday, Ivo has the warehouse audit Thursday, sorry, Friday it is." REFERENCE "- Ivo: place the Barolo order by Friday (he has the warehouse audit Thursday)" passes. Without "(he has the warehouse audit Thursday)" it fails facts_complete: that is the reason for the option kept.
- TRANSCRIPT "quotes came in at $40 a unit for the brochure run and uh I think $61 for the posters". REFERENCE "Quotes came in at $40 a unit for the brochure run and $61 for the posters" fails facts_complete: it dropped "I think".
- TRANSCRIPT "so uh two bags of carrots, no make it three because Joaquin is bringing his kids after all". REFERENCE "- 3 bags of carrots (Joaquin is bringing his kids after all)" passes. "- 3 bags of carrots" fails facts_complete, and "- Three bags of carrots (Joaquin is bringing his kids after all)" fails clean.
- TRANSCRIPT "Tell the crew the Peterson install starts at 8 am. Or you know what, make it 9, the panels won't clear customs until then." REFERENCE "Tell the crew the Peterson install starts at 9 am, since the panels won't clear customs until then." passes. "Tell the crew the Peterson install starts at 8 am. Or you know what, make it 9, the panels won't clear customs until then." fails corrections_right and clean.
- TRANSCRIPT "Ask Freya if we can push the charter to 6 am. Oh but she said the tide's way out then, so hmm, make it 8 instead." REFERENCE "Ask Freya if we can push the charter to 8 am, since she said the tide's way out at 6." passes: the reason stays and still points at 6 am. "Ask Freya if we can push the charter to 8 am, since she said the tide's way out then." fails corrections_right: "then" now points at 8 am.
- REFERENCE "- Down to 6 cases of the Barolo, the 2019 — our best seller" fails clean for the em dash; "- Down to 6 cases of the Barolo, the 2019 (our best seller)" passes.

Return ONLY a JSON object, no prose and no code fence:
{"results": [{"i": 0, "facts_complete": true, "corrections_right": true, "nothing_invented": true, "clean": true, "note": ""}]}

Include one entry per item, with the item's index as "i". For a failing criterion, the note names what is wrong and quotes the words, for example "dropped '2 cans of chickpeas' from the list", "kept 'Tuesday', which the speaker changed to 'Wednesday'" or "'two bags of ice' should be '2 bags of ice'".
