# Correction words used literally

Short dictations where a word people often use to correct themselves is part of what the speaker means, so the cleanup keeps it: "hold on to the receipts until the audit", "never mind the parking, we'll take the train", "can you scratch off the lottery ticket for me", "forget about the deposit, they waived it", "wait for me at the north entrance", "that's wrong, the invoice says forty not fourteen", "sorry I missed your call", "make that call before noon", "change that tire before the trip", "there's a correction in the minutes", "I mean it this time", "we fly out in March, not May".

Cover these words, each in several ways: scratch, never mind, forget, hold on, wait, that's wrong (or that's not right), sorry, make that, change that, correction, I mean, and "not" in a contrast that says what is excluded. Put the word at the very start of the dictation in about a third of the transcripts, since that is where a cleanup most often mistakes it for a correction. In about a third, the speaker also makes one real self-correction somewhere else in the same dictation ("hold on to the receipts until Friday, no, Thursday"), so the cleanup has to tell the two apart. Vary that correction's cue: "no", "actually", "I mean", "sorry", "wait", "make that", or none at all, and sometimes use the same word as the literal one ("wait for me at gate 4, wait, gate 6"). Use a word only where any careful listener would hear it as part of the message, never where it could be the speaker taking something back: "scratch the eggs" in a shopping list is a correction, not this category.

Keep them short: most transcripts are 6 to 25 words, and about one in four runs 26 to 60 words. This replaces the length range above.

Layout is "prose", or "list" for the few that dictate a short list.

Tags: scratch, never_mind, forget, hold_on, wait, thats_wrong, sorry, make_that, change_that, correction, i_mean, not_contrast, cue_first, with_correction.

Example transcript (raw style): "forget about the deposit um they waived it last week"
Cleaned: "Forget about the deposit, they waived it last week."

Example transcript (recognizer style): "Hold on to the, uh, the receipts until Friday, no, Thursday, that's when the auditor comes."
Cleaned: "Hold on to the receipts until Thursday, that's when the auditor comes."
